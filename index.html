Professional and Personality

My school and academic life is a rollercoaster, trying to juggle assignment dealines, test dates, spare time, making time for things I enjoy and keeping my mental health is pretty difficult. My personality traits help me in regards to managing all of those things, Perseverance: Even when things seem too difficult to handle I still try my hardest and eventually things will work out. Patience: Being able to wait until something changes or gets adjusted may be difficult but patience is an amazing trait to have especially academically. Jovial: Being jovial in both good and bad circumstances can prevent someone from going into an emotional slump where their productivity and effectiveness can be negatively affected.

"LLMs such as ChatGPT, Claude, and CoPilot must be used as a tool not a crutch". As we know in this day and age AI is widespread and many types of AI tools are being used daily by all types of persons, however a big issue with the use of AI isn't the AI itself but how it is used. Artificial Intelligence tools such as Claude, ChatGPT and CoPilot are amazing tools that can assist in finding mistakes in essays, equations and code but they're more often used to completely bypass critical thinking or meaningful work. As a student I can understand why teachers have an issue with how AI is used, instead of assisting students with certain tasks students just use it and generate answers and responses to anything they don't want to spend time doing. According to NDSU, some key steps in effective AI usage is compliance with university policies, communication with both tutors and course syllabuses, AI use transparency and using AI as a tool, not a substitute for thinking.

Personal Devlopment

The book "The Richest Man in Babylon" written by George S. Clason, has 11 chapters, but I'll be focusing on two chapters. Firstly "7 Cures for a Lean Purse", it follows a man named Arkad who is the richest man in Babylon in this chapter he teaches people principles that can help them be more financially literate in seven steps. There are two principles that resonate with me heavily, the second and seventh principles are as follows. "Control they Expenditure": Instead of spending without a plan for the funds, do a budget allowing for more control of how much money is spent and encouraging responsibility. The seventh principle "Increase they Ability to earn", this principle simple explains that we should hone our skills, with a wider skillset it will be easier for us to earn income and have multiple sources instead of relying on just one. The second chapter "The Five Laws of Gold", follows the son of Arkad whose name is Nomasir. He learns that having money is futile if a person cannot manage the wealth they have, some parts out of that chapter directly relates to me, I do not save consistently and he states that if someone sets aside 10% of their income their money will grow constantly. Avoiding unrelaistic promises is the other part, when I was younger I set guidlines for my life, having a house by a certain age, a family, a car and other assets but as I got older I realized that putting a date on things like that can force people to resort to illegal ways to get money or fall for schemes like what Nomasir stated, so it's best to take time and secure your future one step at a time
